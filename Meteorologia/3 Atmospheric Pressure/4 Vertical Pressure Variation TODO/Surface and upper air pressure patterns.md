The near surface, presure levels always slope downward oto the low pressure. However depending on the temperature,the pressure levels become progressively modificed with hieight. In some cases the slope of the pressure levels can reverse.

In the case of a surface warm high pressure and surface cold low pressure,the surface pressure level slope towards the low becomes increasingly exaggerated with height by the cold air compacting the isobaric surfaces in the area absove the surface low.

With a surface cold highpressure and surface warm low pressure,the surface pressure slope towards the surface low reverses as the cold air compacts the pressure levels with height.

Just because a high pressure exists on the surface, it does not necessarily mean that there is a low pressure right above in the supper atmoshpere. Of course the pressure in the upper atmosphere is lower than the surface, but the pressure in the upper atmosphere can be higher or lower relative to the air at the same level,  irrespective of surface conditions


El aire **frío comprime** los niveles de presión y el aire **cálido los expande**. Por eso, la temperatura del aire determina si una alta o baja presión de superficie se mantiene, se intensifica o se da la vuelta por completo cuando subes a la alta atmósfera
