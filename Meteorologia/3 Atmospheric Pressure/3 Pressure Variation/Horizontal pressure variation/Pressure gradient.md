Whenever a pressure variation exists,aforce is created that tries to drive air fromthe area of high pressure to the low pressure. This is called the pressure gradient force(PGF). The PGF is the primary force driving the flow of air and,thus,determines the wind speed.

The greater difference in the pressure between two points,the greater the pressure gradient force acting on the air and thus greater wind speed.

Horizontal movement of air is wind. What we are really saying is that horizontal differences in pressure ause wind.

The strenght of the PGFand the resulting wind speed can be found by looking at the spacing of the isobars. Isobars that are very close together show a strong PGF and therefore suggest trong winds,whereas sisobars that are far appartshow a weak PGF and light winds. A slack or flat pressure gradient describes a very low pressure gradient with widely spaced isobars and light winds.

Typically, areas of high-pressure ( a flat pressure pattern) have widely spaced isobars and very light winds.
Areas of low-pressures tend to have isobars that are closer together and therefore stronger winds. However, thi is a very broad generalisation.
