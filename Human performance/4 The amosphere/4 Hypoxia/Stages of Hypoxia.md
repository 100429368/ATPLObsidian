1. **Indiferent**. Extends from MSL to 5 000 ft

2. **Full compenstion**. Automatic changes to greathing and cardiac output compensate adequately for the worst effects of hypoxia. The only adverse effects are on visual dark-adaption and on learning ability. Extends from 5 000 ft to 12 000 ft AMSL. Because of the effects of hypoxia become noticeablein this zone, commercial air transport aircraft are limited to 8 000 ft AMSL.

3. **Incomplete compensation** or clinic
Extends from 11 500 ft to 20 000 ft. In this zone there is an increased riskof acute hypoxia. Symptomslike breathlessness, headache, dizziness, visual disturbances,fatigue,lack of concentration and euphoria. 
Physiological effects become noticeable:
* Peripheral and central vision are impaired, visual acuity decreases.
* Difficulty recognising the emergency situation inless widely experienced with hypoxia and has been very highly traied. Thinking is slow, memoryis fault and judgement is poor
* A release of basic personality traits and emotions. Depending on the individual these can range from euphoria,elation,tomoroseness and pugnacy. The individual may become grossly overconfident. The behaviours are similar to alcohol intoxication.
* Muscular coordination is reduced and the performance of fine or delicate muscular movements may be impossible

4. **Critical**
Begins above 22 000 ft AMSL. Almost complete mental and physical incapacitation, leading to rapid loss of consciousness, convulsions and death.
