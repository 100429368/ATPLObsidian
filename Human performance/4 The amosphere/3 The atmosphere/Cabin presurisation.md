To avoid the need for. Oxygen masks the total airpressure inside the cabin is raised, thus ensuring an adequate partial pressure of oxygen. This is the purpose of the cabin pressurisation system.

Recall that the proportions of the constitutent gases remain constant throughout the range of altitudes used by aircraft. Consequently there is no requierement to add oxygen to the caibin. The only requirement is to increase cabin pressure.

Maintaining cabin pressure at the sea level value would be impractical. The differential pressure between the cabin and the enviroment at altitude would require an extremely strong and heavy fuselage structure.

	Consequently the cabin pressure is allowed to drop progressively to a value equivalent to an altitude of about 6000ft and certainly no more than 8000ft. This is known as the cabin altitude. Although some passengers may notice the effect, the maximum cabin altitude is well below the threshold at which supplemental oxygen is required.

A cabin altitude of 8000ft produces a maximum pressure differential across the fuselage skin of bout 465mm Hg (9psi) at the typical upper flight limits of a commercial airliner
