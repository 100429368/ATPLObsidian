Is the state of breathing faster and/or deeper than necessary for the body's current demand foroxygen. 
Causes:
* Voluntary
* Strokes and brain injuries
* Stress and anxiety

Early symptoms:
* Rapid breathing rate
* Feeling light headed or dizzy
* Tingling lips,hands and feet
* Hot or cold flushes
* Headache
* Impaired performance

Next stage is loss of muscle coodrination and faint.

The reduction in carbonic acid in the blood stream increases blood's alkalinity. This change in pH trigger the autonomic nervous system to constrict blood vessels.The reduced flow of blood to the brain and extremities accounts for all the symptoms of hyperventilation.

Anxiety and stress can trigger the sympathetic nervous system torelease adrenaline which in turn triggers and increase in breathing rate. In its most extreme form this response is commonly called a panic attack.

Methods of overcoming hyperventilation
* Cover the nostril
* Breathing into paper bag to reduce C02
* Speaking loudly, to control breathing rate
