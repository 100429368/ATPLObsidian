Haemoglobin has no particular reference for $O_2$ molecules - Other forms of oxygen are just as easily bond to it. In fact carbon monoxide will bind 200 times more readily with haemoglobin than $O_2$

Once attached to haemoglobin, carbon monoxide poisoning will suffer from hypoxia while displaying no signs of cyanosis; in fact a flushed red face is one of the signs of carbon monoxide poisoning.

The most common source of carbon monoxide is cigarette smoke. Carbon monoxide is also a by-product of the internal combustion engine.
