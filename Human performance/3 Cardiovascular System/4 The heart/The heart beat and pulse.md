The rythmic contraction of the heart muscle is caused,like all other muscle contractions, by an electrical signal. Unlike other muscles which are stimulated by neurons emanating from the centrl nervous system, the heart has its own internal sources of electrical stimulation. The primary in-built placemaker is situated in the wall of the right atrium and produces the normal sinus rhytm. Secondary backup placemakers are located elsewhere in the heart.

Each time a signal is generated by the placemaker,it travels to the heart's musclewhere it spreads from top to bottom to create a highly synchronised contraction of all the individual muscle Cells.

This cuses the pumping action of the heart which in consequence sends a series of pressure waves through the systemiccirculation which we can feel as the pulse.
