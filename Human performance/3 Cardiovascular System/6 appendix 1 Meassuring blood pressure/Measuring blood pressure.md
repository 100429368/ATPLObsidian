The patternof changes in blood pressure in the arteries very closely resemblesthe pattern of blood pressure changes in the heart itself.

Blood pressure can be measured by applying a pressurised cuff to the upper arm. The cuff is progressively tightened until it cuts off the flow of blood in the main arteryof the upper arm. Then it is slowly deflated, just at the point where the peakbloodpressure exceedsthe pressure in the cuff, a distinctive tapping sound can be heard through a stetoscope as blood spurts throught the constricted artery. This gives the reading of **systolic blood pressure**.

As the cuff deflated, the constriction returns to normal flow, then we have the *lower diastolic* value of blood pressure.
