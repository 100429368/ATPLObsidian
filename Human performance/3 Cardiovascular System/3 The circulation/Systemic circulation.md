The arterial supply splits into ever esmaller arterioles and eventually into the tiny capillares which cary oxygenated blood and nutrients to individual cells.

The walls of the capillaries are extremely thin, allowing ocygen and nutrients to fuse readily into the cells and wasteproducts from the cells to diffuse readily back into the blood.
Some of the blood plasma also difusses across the capillary walls to provide the fluid which surrounds all cells and tissues. This fluid drains back into the blood stream via a series of channels knowin as lymphatic system.

To drain the de-oxygenated blood and waste products back to the veins and carry them back to the heart, the capillaries connect up with venules which connect to the larger veins.
