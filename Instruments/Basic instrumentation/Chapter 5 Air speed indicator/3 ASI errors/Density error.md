Density error causes a difference between IAS and TAS wheneverthe actual density of the air is not at the ISA value of 1.225kg/m^3 .This eror is relevant for navigation and planning problems


Ignoring compressibility error,the relationship between IAS and TAS is:

$$Dynamic\space pressure \propto IAS^2\propto \rho TAS^2$$


If a pilot wishes to climb at constant IAS, we mut steadily increase TAS.
IAS= 1/2 rho TAS^2. The greater the reduction in air density,the greater TAS increase required

The correction factor is expressed as:
$$EAS = TAS \sqrt{\frac{\rho}{\rho_0}}$$
It could be simplified as
$$TAS = IAS+ ((IAS/60)\cdot(altitude/1000)$$
***TAS is IAS plus miles per minute times thousands of feet.*
