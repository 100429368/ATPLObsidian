On most CAT aircrafta single Unreliable Airspeed non-normal procedure is setout in the FCOM.

The procedure reliesfirst on comparing all three ASIs. Assuming they all use separate sources, then if two agree they are probably indicating correctly.

Where there are only two pitot-static systems or ADCs,other information must be sought,such as:

* GPS speed. This is groundspeed,only usefull if we know speed.
* Ambien wind nose inside the aircraft
* Pitch and powert settings.  If the aircraft's mass is known, then the combination of pitch and power being used can tellyou a lot about your likely airspeed. This is accurate. 



THREAT
Erroneous ASI reading causing loss of situational awareness and loss of control.
Power + altitude = performance. IFf you level of at a familiar reasonablepitch and power setting the aircraft will stabilise at a reasonable airspeed.
