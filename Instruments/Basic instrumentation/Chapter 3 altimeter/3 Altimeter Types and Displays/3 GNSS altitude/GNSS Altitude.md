GNSS calculate a three dimensional position in space,relative to the positions of the satellite it is using. GNSSaltitude is available on many aicraftbut itsnot used as a reference because of several reasons
1. **Coverage**: GNSS is most accurate when several satellites are close to the overhead. In some areas, for up to 5% of the time, the number of usable satellites may be too low for acceptable accuracy. For altitude indicating even brief service interruptions are unaceptable 
2. **Geometry:** Satellites are selected by the receiver based on being in the best position to provide position accuracy. These satellites will probably not be in a good position to give accurate vertical position accuracy. For this reason typically vertical accuracy bonds are around twice the bounds for lateral accuracy.
3. **Datum**: GNSS altitude is calculated relative to a mathematical model of the Earth, known as geodetic model. It is only an approximation of the mean outline of the Earth, significant iregularities in the shape of the Earth,even overthe sea, are ignored.

In modern CAT aircraft GNSS altitude can be used by the air data systems,along with a database of predicted geodetic error,asa cross-check of system accuracy. 

It is also used as a back-up altimeter for use when all air data sources are unreliable,for example,following a volcanic ash encounter.
