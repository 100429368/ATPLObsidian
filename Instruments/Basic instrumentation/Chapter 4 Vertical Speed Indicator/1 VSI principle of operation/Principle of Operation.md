VSI displays the vertical speedof an aircraft.

It does this by sensing the rate of change of static pressure, comparing the ambient static pressure which is fed directly to a pressure capsule with a delayed value of static pressure fed inside the instrument's case. 
The delay is induced by a metering unit which partially restricts the flow of air in and out the instrument's case.

The capsule is not sealed or partially evacuated. It is connected directly to the staticport. The pressure inside the capsule is the ambient static pressure.

In level flight, pressuresinside the aseand capsuleare the same. The capsule neither contracts or expands.

During a descent or a clumb, in ambient pressure resulting from the change of altitudes causes static pressure in the capsule to change,although there is a slight lag in the reaction of the VSI to these changes. Further,arestritor in the metering unit ensures that the change in static pressure in the case lags slightly behind the change in the capsule.

Differential pressure between the capsule and case causes the casule to expand (if going down)or contract (if going up). The capsule is attached to a linkage mechanism driving the VSI needleto display a rate of descent or rate of climb.


The metering unit restrictor ensures that:
* In a continuous descent, the expanding capsule'spressure is always slightly higher than the case pressure.
* In a continuous climb,the contracting capsule's pressure is always slightly lower than the case pressure.
A high rate of change of ambient pressure in the capsule is displayed as a greater rate of change of altitude(vertical speed)
